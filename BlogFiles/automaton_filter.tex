I had the idea of using Cellular Automata as a post-processing effect implemented it first in the Processing app. Naturally i started
by using the light values of an image to create a mask of live cells, and then iterated them according to the rules of the Game of Life.
\\
After very underwhelming results i concluded that this was not of any interest and abandoned the project. In my later revisit of the topic
i decided to immediately implement the ability to quickly modify the rules, to hopefully discover far more interesting patterns, than the ones
expressed by the Game of Life. I was quickly able to discover a few rule-sets that made interesting images, thus motivating me
to continue this project.
\\\\
This is just a small subset of the possible post-processing effects of Cellular Automata. By considering only the 8 neighbours
surrounding each cell we can find $2^9=512$ rulesets\footnote{Here, $2^9$ comes from the 9 possible values for the number of living cells in the
neighbourhood of any given cell; $0,1,\ldots,8$.} for cell birth, and an additional $512$ for cell survival, giving us a grand total of
$512^2=262.144$ possible rulesets. \\\\
There are many different ways to use Cellular Automata, either by changing the neighbour grid from the Moore 
neighbourhood to the von Neumann neighbourhood (or any other), changing the number of neighbours considered (if we just considered the 3 neighbours above, we 
effectively have a 1D cellular automata, as researched by Wolfram), adding more states than \textit{dead} and \textit{alive}, adding randomness,
or by changing the behaviour of dying and newborn cells. \\
In this excursion I've decided that dying cells take on the average color of its dead neighbours, and newborn cells take on the average 
color of its alive neighbours - letting cells that does not change from alive to dead, or vice versa, keep their color.
